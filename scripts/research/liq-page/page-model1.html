FAQ

How to read the liquidation heatmap, what the colours and marks mean, and where the data comes from.

What is a liquidation heatmap?

Futures are traded with leverage. When price moves far enough against a position, the exchange closes it by force: the position is liquidated. A liquidation heatmap estimates, from the open positions at each price and common leverage settings, how much would be liquidated if price got there. The brighter the colour, the more positions could be liquidated at that price.

Time runs left to right and price bottom to top. Above the current price sit short liquidations, triggered only if price rises; below sit long liquidations, triggered only if it falls. Each column is the estimate at that moment, so you can see when a liquidation zone built up and when it was swept.

These levels matter in two ways. Mass liquidations turn into market orders, so price tends to speed up when it reaches them; and large players like to push price through them to take that liquidity before reversing. The chart is an estimate, not resting orders: once a zone is swept, it darkens and disappears.

For example: a bright band sits above BTC around 66,500, with an estimated short liquidation leverage of 300M. A wick spikes up, the shorts are liquidated into market buys, the band is gone, and price soon falls back.

Reading the chart

What does a brighter colour mean?

Brighter means more positions are estimated to be liquidated if price reaches that level. Brightness is scaled to the liquidation leverage in the current range, the brightest being about the top 0.5% of cells, so compare relative strength within one chart rather than absolute brightness across coins.

Hover over the chart and the tooltip opens a small grid around the cell: three price levels above and below and two columns either side (one of each on a small chart), each in the chart’s colour with its estimated liquidation leverage; the right-hand column is price and the row under the pointer is highlighted. Below the grid are the short and long totals of those cells in this column, then this cell’s liquidation leverage and its distance from the current price.

What is the difference between the zones above and below?

Above the current price are short liquidation levels, triggered only if price rises: shorts are forced to buy back, which pushes price further up. Below are long liquidation levels, triggered if price falls: longs are forced to sell.

The chart splits each column at its close: levels above the close count as shorts, below as longs. So once price rises through a level, that level moves from the short zone to the long zone.

Why does a bright band suddenly disappear?

Price swept through that level: the positions estimated there have been liquidated, later columns no longer carry that liquidation leverage, and the band breaks off at the column where it was swept.

A partial sweep dims it. For example, a band around 66,500 estimated at 300M, hit by a wick to 66,520 that falls back, may hold only about 100M afterwards.

How do I read the liquidation map on the right?

The liquidation map spreads out the latest column: one bar per price level, longer meaning a higher estimated liquidation leverage right now, on the same scale as the price axis.

The two curves are cumulative and start at the current price: the upper one adds up shorts going up, the lower one longs going down. The further right a curve reaches, the more would be swept if price travelled that far. Hover over the map to see a price’s liquidation leverage and the running total.

It answers how much sits on each side now; the main chart answers when it built up. The × at its top right hides it; tick “Liquidation map” under Display in the toolbar to bring it back.

What are the red and green labels on the chart?

These are the key levels: the largest liquidation leverage above and below the current price in the latest column. The red label marks the max short liquidation above, the green one the max long liquidation below; each shows the estimated liquidation leverage, and its price is marked on the price axis in the same colour.

They are off by default: tick “Key levels” under “Display” on the right of the toolbar. Then click a label to open Display again: under “Key levels” choose the price range to look in (±1%, ±3% by default, ±5%, all, or your own %), or untick it to hide the labels and dashed lines. With “Side panel” on, “Key liquidation levels” in it lists four on each side, up to eight when the panel has room beside the chart, each with its distance from price.

What are the labels on the price axis?

The solid label is the current price, with a dashed line across the chart so you can see how far each band is from it. As you move the pointer over the chart, a second label follows it on the axis with the price under the pointer.

Judgement and use

What does Measure add up?

Drag a box on the chart and it is split at the current price: the estimated liquidation leverage in the short zone above and the long zone below is totalled, with the size of each price range in dollars and percent. For example, with price at 65,400 and a box from 65,000 to 67,000, you see how many shorts a rise of about 2.4% would sweep and how many longs a fall of about 0.6% would.

“Right edge” counts only the positions in the box’s right-hand column; “Whole box” adds up every column in it, the liquidations exposed throughout that period.

Will price always go and sweep a bright band?

No. A bright band means many potential liquidations are stacked there: price tends to speed up once it gets there, and large players often use them to take liquidity. Whether and when price gets there depends on the trend, news and the order book.

When both sides are bright, it is hard to tell which one price will visit first; some bands sit untouched for days and slowly fade.

What should I look for across several ranges?

Whether the same price is bright in several ranges. “Key levels across ranges” on the right lines up each panel’s peaks by price; a price that peaks in two or more ranges is marked as a confluence. A zone around 84,700 that is the largest short liquidation zone in 3d, 7d and 30d deserves more attention than one that is bright only in 24h.

Brightness cannot be compared across panels. The longer the range, the more time and price each cell merges, so its liquidation leverage is naturally higher. Each panel’s header shows the liquidation leverage at full brightness: to compare size, read the leverage, not the colours.

Can I trade directly off the liquidation heatmap?

We do not recommend it. The chart is estimated from open positions and common leverage, not real resting orders, and it does not account for changes in trend, news or liquidity; the same bright band can be a target for price or a reversal point.

A better use is to treat bands as alerts: when price gets close, watch for acceleration or a wick, and decide with key levels, larger timeframes and your own stop-loss plan.

Why does the chart change with the range?

The longer the range, the longer each column and the coarser the price levels. At 3 days a column is 15 minutes, at 7 days 30 minutes, at 30 days 2 hours, and at a year about a day; zones that built up and were swept within a short time merge or vanish in long ranges.

Use 12 hours to 3 days for intraday wicks, 30 days and longer for larger liquidation build-ups.

Lenses and tools

What is the difference between the five lenses?

All five lenses draw the same data in different ways. “Heat” draws a block per cell, closest to the raw data; “Bands” merges neighbouring levels into horizontal bands, clearer for the overall layout; “Blocks” draws blocks with gaps, good for reading cell by cell when zoomed in.

“Walls” keeps only the twenty-odd most important liquidation clusters, and “Levels” draws each price level as a line, thicker and brighter for higher liquidation leverage. Switching lenses does not change the colours: pick those under “Colours”.

How do I read “Walls”?

Each bar is one liquidation cluster from build-up to sweep: the left end is when it started forming, the right end when price swept it, or the latest candle if it is still there; thickness and colour show size. Walls still standing are labelled with price and estimated liquidation leverage on the right.

A wall running from long ago to now has sat untouched for a long time; a wall that breaks off where price crossed it was swept then.

What are “Colours” and “Long / short colours” for?

“Colours” changes only the colours, not the drawing: the nine palettes work in every lens, so pick what suits your screen; the last three use two colours, one above the price and one below. Turning on “Long / short colours” uses two colours instead, one for the short zone and one for the long zone, so the sides are easy to tell apart; the palette picker greys out then.

How do I set the intensity range?

Drag the left handle and cells weaker than that intensity are hidden, which removes scattered small liquidations; drag the right handle and everything stronger is drawn at full brightness, which brings out mid-sized zones. Drag the middle to move both, and click “Reset” to go back to the default.

The numbers beside the handles are converted to a single cell’s liquidation leverage: “≥ 15% · 7.7M” means only cells with an estimated liquidation leverage above 7.7M are shown.

Can price be shown as a line?

Yes. Click “Display” on the right of the toolbar and choose “Candles”, “Line” or “Hidden” under “Price”. When zoomed out to less than a pixel per column, candles turn into a line by themselves; choose “Hidden” to focus on the liquidation layout.

What is the difference between Model 1, 2 and 3?

The three models suit different time frames and draw on different data sources. Model 1 suits short time frames: intraday moves and liquidation zones a few days out. Model 2 sits between the two and adds more data sources than Model 1. Model 3 suits long time frames and adds the most data sources, for larger liquidation zones over weeks to months.

Switch with “Model 1”, “Model 2” and “Model 3” at the top; the chart is redrawn with the chosen model’s data.

How do I watch several ranges at once?

Under “Layout” in the toolbar choose “Two side by side”, “Two stacked” or “Four-panel grid”. Each panel is one range of the same coin, 24h, 3d, 7d and 30d by default; change a panel’s range with the picker at its top left.

“Link prices” is on by default: every panel uses the same price range, so a price sits at the same height in each, and hovering over one panel draws that price on the others. The wheel zooms a panel’s time; hold Shift and scroll to zoom price. Double-click a panel’s header or click the button at its top right to enlarge it, and press Esc to go back to the grid.

What does “Screenshot” make?

The chart as you see it, redrawn at 3× resolution so it stays sharp when enlarged. On top are the coin or pair and range, the model, lens and data time, and the last price; the bottom line gives the source, so anyone you send it to knows what they are looking at.

In the preview click “Copy image” and paste it into a chat or document, or click “Download PNG” to save it. If the browser does not allow clipboard access, right-click the image to copy it.

Data and controls

Where does the data come from, and how often is it updated?

Prices and candles come from the exchanges’ trades. Liquidation prices and leverage are estimated from each exchange’s open interest (OI) and common leverage settings: the brightness and liquidation leverage on the chart are estimates.

The data is fetched when you open the page or change coin, range or model; Prime members’ charts refresh every 30 seconds. The status in the toolbar shows when the data on screen was updated.

Which coins and pairs are covered?

“Coin” aggregates the whole market: the same coin’s perpetuals across the major exchanges combined. “Pair” shows one exchange’s perpetual, with the exchange on each row of the picker; Model 3 covers Binance, OKX, Bybit, Bitget and Hyperliquid.

There are many pairs: type to search, e.g. “okx btc” or “bybit eth”. The title says which coin, or which exchange’s contract, you are looking at.

How do I use the chart?

Drag to pan; the wheel zooms around the pointer, and when zoomed in far enough each cell shows its estimated liquidation leverage. Hold Shift and scroll to zoom only price, Alt to zoom only time; dragging the price axis on the right or the time axis at the bottom zooms that axis too, as do the “+” and “−” buttons under the chart; double-click or press R to see everything. Hold Shift and drag to measure a box, press S to switch between Pan and Measure, and Esc to clear the box. On a phone, drag sideways to pan and pinch to zoom.

Lens, colours, layout and the Display options are remembered in your browser; theme and language follow the site.
